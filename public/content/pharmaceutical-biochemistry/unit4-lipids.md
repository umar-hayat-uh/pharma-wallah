## Core Content

### 3.1 Introduction to Enzymes

#### Definition
Enzymes are biological catalysts—typically proteins (with the exception of some catalytic RNA molecules called ribozymes)—that increase the rate of chemical reactions in living organisms without being consumed or permanently altered in the process.

#### Key Characteristics
- **Specificity:** Enzymes are highly specific for their substrates.
- **Efficiency:** Enzymes accelerate reaction rates by factors of 10⁶ to 10¹² compared to uncatalyzed reactions.
- **Regulation:** Enzyme activity is tightly controlled to meet cellular needs.
- **Mild Conditions:** Enzymes function under physiological conditions (temperature ~37°C, pH ~7.4).

#### Historical Background
- 1833: Payen and Persoz discovered the first enzyme, diastase (amylase).
- 1878: Kühne coined the term "enzyme" (from Greek: "in yeast").
- 1894: Emil Fischer proposed the "Lock and Key" model of enzyme-substrate specificity.
- 1926: Sumner first crystallized urease and proved enzymes are proteins (Nobel Prize in 1946).
- 1948: Pauling proposed the concept of transition state stabilization.
- 1958: Koshland proposed the "Induced Fit" model.

#### Chemical Nature of Enzymes

| Type | Composition | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Simple Enzymes | Composed entirely of protein | Pepsin, Trypsin, Lysozyme |
| Conjugated Enzymes | Protein + Non-protein prosthetic group | NAD⁺-dependent dehydrogenases, Flavoproteins |
| Holoenzyme | Apoenzyme (protein) + Cofactor (non-protein) | Lactate dehydrogenase (NAD⁺) |
| Apoenzyme | The protein portion without cofactor | Inactive form |
| Cofactor | Non-protein component (inorganic) | Zn²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺, Cu²⁺ |
| Coenzyme | Organic non-protein component | NAD⁺, FAD, Coenzyme A, TPP |
| Prosthetic Group | Tightly bound coenzyme | Heme, FAD (in succinate dehydrogenase) |

#### Structure of Enzymes
- **Active Site:** A small region (3‑15 amino acids) where substrate binding and catalysis occur.
  - Contains catalytic residues that participate in bond breaking/forming.
  - Often contains metal ions for catalytic function.
- **Allosteric Site:** A distinct site where regulatory molecules (modulators) bind.
  - Binding induces conformational changes that increase or decrease activity.
- **Substrate Binding Sites:**
  - **Lock and Key Model (Fischer, 1894):** The active site has a rigid shape exactly complementary to the substrate.
  - **Induced Fit Model (Koshland, 1958):** The active site changes conformation upon substrate binding to achieve optimal fit.
- **Transition State Stabilization:**
  - Enzymes stabilize the high-energy transition state, lowering the activation energy (ΔG°‡).
  - The enzyme does not change the equilibrium constant (Kₑq) but dramatically increases the rate of reaching equilibrium.

### 3.2 Classification of Enzymes

The Enzyme Commission (EC) of the IUBMB classifies enzymes into 6 major classes based on the type of reaction they catalyze:

| EC Number | Class | Reaction Type | Examples |
|-----------|-------|---------------|----------|
| 1 | Oxidoreductases | Transfer of electrons (redox reactions) | Alcohol dehydrogenase, Lactate dehydrogenase, Cytochrome oxidase |
| 2 | Transferases | Transfer of functional groups | Hexokinase, Aspartate aminotransferase |
| 3 | Hydrolases | Hydrolysis (bond cleavage with H₂O) | Amylase, Lipase, Pepsin, Trypsin, Acetylcholinesterase |
| 4 | Lyases | Non-hydrolytic cleavage or addition | Fumarase, Aldolase, Decarboxylases |
| 5 | Isomerases | Intramolecular rearrangement | Phosphoglucose isomerase, Triose phosphate isomerase, Epimerases |
| 6 | Ligases (Synthetases) | Bond formation coupled with ATP hydrolysis | DNA ligase, Acetyl-CoA carboxylase, Glutamine synthetase |

### 3.3 Enzyme Specificity

| Type of Specificity | Description | Example |
|---------------------|-------------|---------|
| Absolute Specificity | Acts on only one substrate | Urease (hydrolyzes only urea) |
| Group Specificity | Acts on substrates with a specific group | Trypsin (hydrolyzes Arg/Lys peptide bonds) |
| Linkage Specificity | Acts on a specific type of bond | Glycosidases (hydrolyze glycosidic bonds) |
| Geometric (Optical) Specificity | Acts on one stereoisomer (D or L) | L-amino acid oxidase (acts only on L-amino acids) |
| Co-factor Specificity | Requires specific cofactor | Many dehydrogenases require NAD⁺ or NADP⁺ |

### 3.4 Mechanism of Enzyme Action

#### General Steps
1. **Substrate Binding:** Substrate (S) binds to the active site, forming an enzyme-substrate complex (ES).
2. **Transition State Formation:** The enzyme stabilizes the transition state, lowering activation energy.
3. **Catalysis:** Chemical bonds are made/broken; product (P) is formed.
4. **Product Release:** Product is released, and the enzyme is regenerated.

**Equation:** E + S ⇌ ES → E + P

#### Factors Contributing to Enzyme Catalysis

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Proximity and Orientation | Substrates are brought together in the correct orientation at the active site. |
| Strain and Distortion | Binding induces strain in the substrate; bonds are easier to break. |
| Acid-Base Catalysis | Amino acid residues (e.g., His, Asp, Glu) donate/accept protons. |
| Covalent Catalysis | Formation of transient covalent enzyme-substrate bonds (e.g., serine proteases). |
| Metal Ion Catalysis | Metal ions (Zn²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺) participate in redox or electrostatic stabilization. |
| Low-Barrier Hydrogen Bonds | Short strong hydrogen bonds stabilize the transition state. |

### 3.5 Enzyme Kinetics

#### 3.5.1 Michaelis-Menten Equation

**Definition:** The Michaelis-Menten equation describes the relationship between substrate concentration [S] and reaction velocity (V).

**Assumptions:**
- Single substrate reaction (or no inhibition).
- Steady-state assumption: [ES] is constant (d[ES]/dt = 0).
- Total enzyme concentration [E]ₜ is constant.
- Reaction is irreversible (at least initially).

**Derivation:**  
E + S ⇌ (k₁, k₋₁) ES → (k₂) E + P

**Rate Equations:**
- Rate of formation of ES: k₁[E][S] + k₋₂[E][P]
- Rate of breakdown of ES: k₋₁[ES] + k₂[ES]

**Steady-State (simplified):**  
k₁[E][S] = (k₋₁ + k₂)[ES]

**Michaelis-Menten Equation:**  
V₀ = (V_max × [S]) / (K_m + [S])

where:
- V₀ = Initial velocity
- V_max = Maximum velocity (at saturating [S])
- [S] = Substrate concentration
- K_m = Michaelis constant = (k₋₁ + k₂)/k₁

| Parameter | Definition | Significance |
|-----------|------------|--------------|
| K_m | Substrate concentration at which V₀ = ½ V_max | Measure of affinity: lower K_m = higher affinity |
| V_max | Maximum reaction velocity at saturating [S] | Proportional to total enzyme concentration |
| K_cat (Turnover Number) | V_max / [E]ₜ | Number of substrate molecules converted per enzyme molecule per second |
| Specificity Constant | K_cat / K_m | Efficiency of the enzyme; measures both rate and affinity |

#### 3.5.2 Lineweaver-Burk Plot (Double Reciprocal Plot)

Double reciprocal equation:  
1/V₀ = (K_m/V_max) × (1/[S]) + 1/V_max

**Plot:** 1/V₀ (y-axis) vs 1/[S] (x-axis) → a straight line.

**Advantages:**
- Easy to determine V_max and K_m.
- Useful for identifying types of enzyme inhibition.

### 3.6 Enzyme Inhibition

**Definition:** Enzyme inhibition is the decrease in enzyme activity caused by inhibitors (molecules that bind to the enzyme and reduce its catalytic activity).

#### 3.6.1 Types of Inhibition

**A. Reversible Inhibition (Inhibitor binds non-covalently; activity can be restored)**

| Type | Binding Site | Effect on V_max | Effect on K_m | Lineweaver-Burk Plot | Example |
|------|--------------|-----------------|---------------|----------------------|---------|
| Competitive Inhibition | Active site (competes with substrate) | Unchanged (↓ V_max apparent) | ↑ K_m (apparent) | Lines intersect on y-axis | Statins (HMG-CoA reductase), Methotrexate (DHFR) |
| Non-Competitive Inhibition | Allosteric site (different from active site) | ↓ V_max (apparent) | Unchanged (K_m same) | Lines intersect on x-axis | Heavy metals, Some antitumor agents |
| Uncompetitive Inhibition | Only binds to ES complex | ↓ V_max (apparent) | ↓ K_m (apparent) | Lines are parallel | Some antidepressants, Cations |
| Mixed Inhibition | Allosteric site; affects both binding and catalysis | ↓ V_max | ↑ or ↓ K_m | Lines intersect between axes | Many clinically relevant inhibitors |

**Competitive Inhibition Details:**
- Overcome by high substrate concentration ([S]).
- Examples: Statins (HMG-CoA reductase), Methotrexate (dihydrofolate reductase), Sulfonamides (p-aminobenzoic acid analogues), Malonate (succinate dehydrogenase).

**Non-Competitive Inhibition Details:**
- Cannot be overcome by high [S].
- Examples: Heavy metal ions (Pb²⁺, Hg²⁺) binding to sulfhydryl groups; DIPF (diisopropyl fluorophosphate) irreversibly inhibits serine proteases.

**Uncompetitive Inhibition Details:**
- Rare; inhibitor only binds to ES complex.
- Examples: Some inhibitors of alkaline phosphatase; Lithium (in some reactions).

**B. Irreversible Inhibition**
- Inhibitor covalently binds to enzymes (permanent inactivation).
- Examples:
  - Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid): irreversibly acetylates cyclooxygenase (COX) → inhibits prostaglandin synthesis.
  - Penicillin: irreversibly inhibits bacterial transpeptidase (cell wall synthesis).
  - Organophosphates (Sarin, Malathion): irreversibly inhibit acetylcholinesterase (→ nerve gas poisoning).
  - DIPF: inhibits serine proteases.

### 3.7 Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

#### 3.7.1 Substrate Concentration ([S])
- As [S] increases, V₀ increases until V_max is reached (saturation).
- Equation: V₀ = (V_max × [S]) / (K_m + [S])

#### 3.7.2 Enzyme Concentration ([E])
- Proportionality: V₀ ∝ [E] (under substrate saturating conditions).
- This is the basis for enzyme assays and quantification.

#### 3.7.3 Temperature
- **Optimum Temperature:** Most mammalian enzymes ~37°C.
- **Effect of Heat:**
  - Below optimum: V increases (more kinetic energy).
  - At optimum: Peak activity.
  - Above optimum: Denaturation (loss of tertiary/quaternary structure).
- **Temperature Coefficient (Q₁₀):** For most enzymes, V doubles for every 10°C increase.

#### 3.7.4 pH
- **Optimum pH:** Varies per enzyme (e.g., pepsin pH 1.5, trypsin pH 8.0, alkaline phosphatase pH 9-10).
- **Effect:** Changes in ionization of active site residues (histidine, cysteine, lysine) affect substrate binding and catalysis.
- Enzymes have a narrow pH range (denature at extremes).

#### 3.7.5 Activators and Inhibitors
- **Activators:** Ions (Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, Zn²⁺) or small molecules that increase activity.
- **Inhibitors:** As described above (competitive, non-competitive, irreversible).

#### 3.7.6 Effect of Product Accumulation
- Feedback inhibition: product accumulation inhibits the first enzyme in a pathway.

### 3.8 Allosteric Enzymes

#### Definition
Allosteric enzymes are regulatory enzymes that have binding sites for effector molecules (allosteric modulators) distinct from the active site. Binding of effectors induces conformational changes that alter enzyme activity.

#### Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Regulation | Activity modulated by non-covalent binding of small molecules. |
| Substrate Binding | Often exhibit sigmoidal (S-shaped) kinetics instead of hyperbolic (Michaelis-Menten). |
| Cooperativity | Binding of one substrate/effector affects the binding of subsequent molecules. |
| Multiple Subunits | Allosteric enzymes are usually composed of multiple subunits (quaternary structure). |
| Role | Control of metabolic pathways (rate-limiting steps). |

#### Kinetic Behavior
- **Sigmoidal Kinetics:** The curve of V₀ vs [S] is S-shaped.
  - At low [S]: binding is less cooperative (low affinity).
  - At high [S]: cooperativity increases (high affinity).
- **Significance:** Sigmoidal kinetics allows a rapid, switch-like response to changes in substrate or effector concentrations.

#### Types of Allosteric Modulators

| Type | Effect | Examples |
|------|--------|----------|
| Positive Modulators (Activators) | Increase V_max (or decrease K_m) | AMP activates Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1); Acetyl-CoA activates Pyruvate Carboxylase; Ca²⁺ activates Glycogen Phosphorylase |
| Negative Modulators (Inhibitors) | Decrease V_max (or increase K_m) | ATP inhibits PFK-1; Citrate inhibits PFK-1; Cholesterol inhibits HMG-CoA Reductase; Glucose-6-P inhibits Glycogen Phosphorylase |

#### Clinical/Pharmaceutical Importance of Allosteric Enzymes

| Allosteric Enzyme | Pathway/Process | Clinical Significance |
|-------------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| PFK-1 | Glycolysis | Key regulatory enzyme in glycolysis; regulated by ATP, AMP, citrate, fructose-2,6-bisphosphate |
| HMG-CoA Reductase | Cholesterol synthesis | Allosteric regulation by cholesterol; target of statins (competitive inhibitor) |
| Glucokinase | Glycolysis | Allosteric inhibitor of fructose-1-P; defects lead to MODY (Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young) |
| Glycogen Synthase | Glycogenesis | Activated by glucose-6-P; inhibited by phosphorylation (insulin activates) |
| Glutamine Synthetase | Nitrogen metabolism | Inhibited by multiple end products (cumulative feedback inhibition) |
| Protein Kinase A (cAMP-dependent) | Signal transduction | Allosteric activation by cAMP (release of regulatory subunit) |
| PKM2 (Pyruvate Kinase M2) | Glycolysis | Allosteric activation by fructose-1,6-bisphosphate; altered in cancer (Warburg effect) |

#### Models of Allosteric Regulation

**1. Concerted Model (Monod-Wyman-Changeux, MWC Model):**
- All subunits exist in either R (Relaxed, active) or T (Tensed, inactive) states.
- All subunits switch simultaneously (concerted).
- Substrate binds preferentially to R state.
- Effectors:
  - Positive: Stabilize R state.
  - Negative: Stabilize T state.

**2. Sequential Model (Koshland-Némethy-Filmer, KNF Model):**
- Subunits change conformation individually upon ligand binding.
- Conformational changes propagate to neighboring subunits.
- Cooperativity arises from progressive conformational changes.

### 3.11 Mechanism of Action of Important Enzymes

#### 3.11.1 Chymotrypsin
**Definition:** Chymotrypsin is a serine protease (EC 3.4.21.1) that hydrolyzes peptide bonds adjacent to hydrophobic amino acids (Phe, Tyr, Trp, Leu, Met).
**Source:** Pancreatic secretions; synthesized as an inactive precursor (zymogen) chymotrypsinogen, activated by trypsin.

**Structure:**
- Single polypeptide chain (245 amino acids) with a catalytic triad.
- **Catalytic Triad:** Ser195, His57, Asp102.
- **Oxyanion hole** stabilizes the transition state.

**Mechanism of Action (Covalent Catalysis - Two-step Mechanism):**

*Step 1: Acylation:*
1. Binding: Substrate binds to the active site; hydrophobic pocket accommodates the aromatic/large hydrophobic side chain.
2. Nucleophilic Attack: Ser195 Oγ attacks the peptide carbonyl carbon.
3. Transition State Stabilization: Oxyanion hole forms hydrogen bonds with the carbonyl oxygen.
4. Hydrolysis: His57 abstracts proton from Ser195; tetrahedral intermediate forms.
5. Covalent Bond Formation: Ser195 acylates the substrate (acyl-enzyme intermediate).
6. Amino Group Release: The new N-terminus of the amino fragment is released via deacylation.

*Step 2: Deacylation:*
1. Water Molecule: Water enters the active site.
2. Hydrolysis: His57 deprotonates water; water attacks the acyl-enzyme intermediate.
3. Release: The acyl group is released as the carboxylic acid fragment; enzyme is regenerated.

**Key Interactions:**
- **Specificity Pocket:** Deep, lined with hydrophobic residues (Gly216, Ser189, Asp189) that accommodate aromatic/hydrophobic side chains.
- **Oxyanion Hole:** Stabilizes the transition state by hydrogen bonding.
- **Catalytic Triad:** Asp102 stabilizes His57, allowing it to abstract/give protons.

**Substrates:** Peptides with aromatic (Phe, Tyr, Trp) or bulky hydrophobic (Leu, Met) amino acids.

**Inhibitors:**
- Irreversible: DIPF (diisopropyl fluorophosphate) covalently binds to Ser195 (suicide inhibitor).
- Reversible: Aprotinin (pancreatic trypsin inhibitor), TPCK (specific for chymotrypsin).
- Clinical: Protease inhibitors are used for pancreatitis, bleeding disorders.

#### 3.11.2 Ribonuclease (RNase A)
**Definition:** Ribonuclease A (RNase A, EC 4.6.1.19) is an endonuclease that cleaves single-stranded RNA by hydrolyzing the phosphodiester bond adjacent to pyrimidine nucleotides (cytosine, uracil).
**Source:** Bovine pancreas.

**Structure:**
- Single polypeptide chain (124 amino acids) with four disulfide bonds.
- **Catalytic Residues:** His12, His119, Lys41 (critical for catalysis).
- **Pyrimidine-specific binding pocket:** Contains Thr45, Phe120, Ser123.

**Mechanism of Action (Acid-Base Catalysis - Two-step Mechanism):**

*Step 1: Transphosphorylation (Cleavage of RNA chain):*
1. Substrate Binding: RNA binds; pyrimidine base (U or C) is positioned in the specificity pocket.
2. Nucleophilic Attack: 2'-OH of ribose (immediately adjacent to the phosphate) attacks the phosphorus atom.
3. Acid-Base Catalysis: His12 acts as a base (abstracts proton from 2'-OH); His119 acts as an acid (donates proton to 5'-O leaving group).
4. Pentavalent Intermediate: Trigonal bipyramidal transition state.
5. Cleavage: 2',3'-cyclic phosphate intermediate is formed; 5'-OH product is released.

*Step 2: Hydrolysis (Degradation of cyclic phosphate):*
1. Water Molecule: Water enters the active site.
2. Nucleophilic Attack: Water (activated by His119) attacks the cyclic phosphate.
3. Ring Opening: 2',3'-cyclic phosphate is opened to yield 3'-phosphate end.
4. Release: 3'-phosphate nucleotide is released; enzyme is regenerated.

**Key Interactions:**
- **Specificity Pocket:** Binds pyrimidine bases (C and U) via hydrogen bonds (Thr45, Phe120, Ser123).
- **Catalytic Residues:** His12 and His119 carry out acid-base catalysis.
- **Lys41:** Stabilizes the phosphate group via electrostatic interactions.
- **Disulfide Bonds:** Stabilize the enzyme structure (RNase A has four disulfide bonds).

**Substrates:** Single-stranded RNA; cleaves after pyrimidine nucleotides (U or C).

**Inhibitors:**
- Non-specific: Heavy metal ions, ribonuclease inhibitor (protein).
- Specific: Uridine 2',3'-cyclic phosphate (transition state analogue).

**Significance of RNase A:**
- Classical Protein Folding Model: RNase A was one of the first proteins to be refolded in vitro (Anfinsen's experiment; Nobel Prize 1972).
- Catalytic Mechanism: A textbook example of acid-base catalysis.
- Pharmaceutical Relevance: RNases are being studied as anti-cancer agents (angiogenin).

### 3.12 Enzyme Regulation

| Regulatory Mechanism | Description | Examples |
|----------------------|-------------|----------|
| Allosteric Regulation | Modulators bind at allosteric sites; induce conformational changes | PFK-1 (ATP inhibits, AMP activates) |
| Covalent Modification | Reversible phosphorylation/dephosphorylation (kinases/phosphatases) | Glycogen phosphorylase (phosphorylated = active); Glycogen synthase (phosphorylated = inactive) |
| Zymogen (Proenzyme) Activation | Inactive precursor activated by proteolytic cleavage | Pepsinogen → Pepsin; Trypsinogen → Trypsin; Chymotrypsinogen → Chymotrypsin; Prothrombin → Thrombin |
| Isozymes (Isoenzymes) | Different forms of the same enzyme with different kinetic properties | LDH (LDH-1 heart, LDH-5 muscle); CK (CK-MM muscle, CK-MB heart) |
| Induction/Repression of Gene Expression | Synthesis or degradation of enzyme molecules | Insulin induces glucokinase; Drugs induce CYP450 enzymes |
| Feedback Inhibition | End product of a pathway inhibits the first enzyme | Cholesterol inhibits HMG-CoA reductase; CTP inhibits ATCase; Glucose-6-P inhibits hexokinase |
| Substrate Availability | Enzyme activity limited by substrate concentration | Michaelis-Menten kinetics; K_m determines affinity |

### Key Points Frequently Tested
- **Michaelis-Menten:** V₀ = (V_max × [S]) / (K_m + [S]); K_m = affinity; V_max = [E]ₜ × k_cat.
- **Inhibitors:** Competitive (↑K_m, V_max unchanged), Non-competitive (↓V_max, K_m unchanged), Uncompetitive (↓both), Irreversible (permanent inhibition).

## 6. Summary Box
- Enzymes are protein catalysts that accelerate biochemical reactions with high specificity and efficiency.
- Enzyme classification (EC system): 6 major classes—oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, and ligases.
- Michaelis-Menten kinetics describes the relationship between substrate concentration and reaction velocity (V₀ = V_max[S]/(K_m+[S])); K_m indicates affinity.
- Enzyme inhibition: Competitive (↑K_m), non-competitive (↓V_max), uncompetitive (↓both), and irreversible (permanent; drugs like aspirin, penicillin).
- Allosteric enzymes have regulatory sites; they display sigmoidal kinetics and are regulated by modulators (activators/inhibitors) that control metabolic pathways.
- Enzymes are vital in metabolism, DNA replication, signal transduction, and pharmaceutical drug action and diagnosis.
- Chymotrypsin is a serine protease with a catalytic triad (Ser-His-Asp) that uses covalent and acid-base catalysis. Ribonuclease A uses acid-base catalysis with His12 and His119 to cleave RNA at pyrimidine nucleotides.
